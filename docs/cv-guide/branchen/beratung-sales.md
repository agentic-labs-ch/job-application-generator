# Beratung & Sales (consulting, presales, account management, B2B sales)

Researched 2026-09-27 for the CV generator. Rules for CVs of other people in this Branche; `docs/cv-guide.md` stays the base rule set. Sources are listed at the end, numbered [1], [2] …

What this Branche has in common: the CV is itself a sales document. The reader wants a clear
claim (what this person is), three strengths with proof, and numbers that show scale and
results. Job ads are referenced by code (M1–M4, I1–I4, P1–P4, A1–A4); they are listed in the
table at the end of each sub-sector section.

## How readers in this Branche read a CV

- First read is a fast scan. An eye-tracking study found recruiters spend 7.4 seconds on the
  first pass; simple layouts with clear headings did well, multiple columns and long sentences
  did badly [7]. The ETH guide cites the earlier 6-second result: 80 % of that time goes to
  name, current title, previous employer, the dates of the current and previous position, and
  education [1].
  HSG puts the first review at 30 seconds to one minute [2].
- Most CVs are dropped at this stage. For top consulting firms, "80% of the applications will
  be eliminated" at the first glance (CaseCoach [10]).
- Who reads: a recruiter or HR screener first, who checks the CV against the job description
  [12]; then the hiring manager (partner, practice lead, sales director). In sales and presales
  the first recruiter scan looks for the sales vocabulary of the ad (demo, PoC, RFP, quota).
- ATS: many large companies, and more and more mid-sized ones, use an applicant tracking
  system (Bewerbermanagement-System) that can filter by keywords, skills, previous employers
  and experience [1]. Hays Switzerland
  and Accenture both tell applicants to reuse the keywords of the ad [4][13].
- What they check first: seniority, sector experience and functional knowledge, systems and
  language skills should be "immediately evident" (Michael Page Switzerland [17]). Sector
  experience "can really tip the balance" [5].
- Consulting firms also check the academic record and full transcripts. BCG Switzerland asks
  for the CV, gymnasium (Matura) transcripts, all university transcripts and references [8];
  Bain asks for relevant test scores where applicable [9].
- Red flags named by Deloitte Switzerland recruiters: spelling mistakes, poor formatting, no
  summary of tasks per position, very long CVs, unexplained gaps [12].

## Common to all sub-sectors

### Length and format

| Topic | Rule for Switzerland | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| Pages | At most 2 A4 pages. Consulting graduates: 1 page is the safe default; experienced hires may use 2. | [1][2][3][4][10][11] |
| Photo | Customary in German-speaking Switzerland, never mandatory. Professional photo only. Global consulting and US vendors often expect no photo in English-language applications: make the photo a per-application switch. | [1][3][4][10][11] |
| Dates | Month and year, newest first. The project format `MM.YYYY – MM.YYYY` fits. | [1][2] |
| Languages | Name the level: CEFR (A1–C2) or a descriptive term (Muttersprache, verhandlungssicher, fliessend). Order by relevance, mother tongue first. Ads here often demand German and English, sometimes with a CEFR level (M2: German C1, English B2). | [1][3] |
| File | A clean PDF (Hays: PDF rather than Word); some firms also accept Word. | [4][5][9] |
| Personal data | Place of residence and work permit if relevant; no civil status, religion or children. The project's privacy rules apply. | [3] |
| Enclosures | Arbeitszeugnisse and diplomas are expected in Swiss applications; consulting firms add transcripts. | [8][12] |

### Section order

1. Header: name, target role, one-line claim (positioning headline). Accenture calls this a
   "trigger summary" of one or two sentences [13].
2. Profil (Kurzprofil): three to four sentences, tailored to the ad; not a repeat of the CV
   body. ETH notes it is common but not standard in Swiss CVs [1]. In this Branche it carries
   the claim and the strongest number; on a one-page consulting CV it can shrink to one line.
3. Kernkompetenzen: three strengths, each with one line of proof (base guide, section 2).
4. Berufserfahrung: newest first, results before tasks.
5. Ausbildung. Consulting graduates move this to position 3 (see sub-sector 1).
6. Weiterbildung & Zertifikate. IT consulting and presales move this up (see sub-sectors 2
   and 3).
7. Sprachen, then tools and methods (Weitere Kenntnisse), then personal details and interests.

### Tone and verbs

- Impersonal style, no "I" or "we" [5][6]. Past tense for past positions, present tense for
  the current one.
- Specific and fact-based: "quantify and qualify" (Harvard [6]). Each point should say what
  was done, the scale and the result.
- Verbs from the Harvard list (in `docs/cv-guide.md`) that fit this Branche:

| Use | Harvard verbs that fit |
|---|---|
| Analysis and advice | Analyzed, Diagnosed, Evaluated, Identified, Investigated, Modeled, Recommended, Synthesized, Structured, Forecasted |
| Selling and persuading | Negotiated, Persuaded, Convinced, Influenced, Presented, Promoted, Sold |
| Growth and results | Increased, Expanded, Generated, Launched, Surpassed, Accelerated, Reduced, Maximized, Strengthened |
| Leading delivery | Led, Directed, Orchestrated, Coordinated, Delivered, Executed, Steered, Implemented |
| Enablement | Coached, Trained, Facilitated, Advised, Demonstrated |

The list is in American spelling; in English CVs follow the project's spelling ("analysed",
"modelled"), as the base guide says. "Led", "Sold" and "Generated" need a matching role or
number in the dataset.

- Harvard verbs to avoid here: artistic or odd ones (Acted, Composed, Fashioned, Energized,
  Verbalized, Rehabilitated, Arbitrated, Fabricated, Remodeled) and weak ones that hide the
  person's part (Assisted, Participated, Handled, Utilized, Provided). "Helped" and
  "responsible for" read as duties, not results.
- Extra verbs typical in this Branche (not on the Harvard list): Won, Closed, Exceeded,
  Prospected, Qualified (pipeline), Scoped, Pitched, Renewed, Upsold, Cross-sold, Onboarded,
  Architected, Prototyped, Benchmarked, Quantified, Sized (a market).
- German verbs seen in Swiss ads and suitable for bullets: verantwortet, gewonnen,
  abgeschlossen, ausgebaut, gesteigert, gesenkt, erarbeitet, konzipiert, präsentiert,
  verhandelt, eingeführt, geleitet, erschlossen (new markets, A3).

### Numbers to show

| Type | Fictional example |
|---|---|
| Revenue or bookings (CHF) | CHF 2.4 million new annual contract value |
| Target attainment | 118 % of the 2025 quota; rank 2 of 14 |
| Growth | Account revenue from CHF 8 million to CHF 10.5 million in two years |
| Savings or value found | CHF 6 million annual savings identified |
| Win rate and conversion | 6 of 8 proofs of concept converted |
| Scope and scale | 140 applications, 3,000 users, 12 countries |
| Team and budget | Team of six; project budget CHF 1.2 million |
| Time | Go-live in 9 instead of 12 months |
| Level of audience | Presented to the executive board (Geschäftsleitung) or board of directors (Verwaltungsrat) |
| Retention | 96 % gross revenue retention on 120 SME customers |

- Give context for each number: period, base, territory or team size. "133% of quota in 2024"
  is more believable than "above quota" (Sales Talent [15]).
- Swiss number format in German texts: apostrophe as thousands separator (3'000), "Mio." for
  millions (CHF 4 Mio.), a space before "%" (18 %). English texts: 3,000 and CHF 4 million.
  Where a dataset or reference writes numbers differently, the reference wins (base guide).
- Consulting and presales work is often under confidentiality. Describe the client by type,
  size and sector ("a Swiss private bank", "a cantonal utility") unless the name is public and
  approved.

### What to avoid

- Duty lists without outcomes, vague claims, AI-sounding filler, jargon, and anything that
  cannot be defended in the interview [11]. Lying about skills "won't stand up to the test" [5].
- Unexplained gaps and overlong CVs [12]; spelling mistakes [5][6][12].
- ATS traps: contact details only in the page header, unusual fonts, logos, tables for layout,
  skill-level dots or bars [1]. Clean layouts without logos, pictures or graphs are the norm in
  consulting [10].
- "Strategic" hobbies chosen only to look good [1].
- Awards buried at the end: a sales award or ranking belongs with the role where it was earned
  [15].

## 1. Management consulting (strategy consulting)

Covers MBB (McKinsey, BCG, Bain), the Big Four strategy arms (EY-Parthenon, Deloitte, Strategy&)
and Swiss or DACH boutiques. In Switzerland many Zurich boutiques recruit in German; global
firms interview in German or French with at least one interview in English [8].

### What recruiters expect

- Education: a Master's or MBA from a leading school "with excellent results" (M1); "excellent
  academic performance" (M3, M4). Full transcripts from the Matura on (BCG [8]). Grades from
  another system: show the scale (5.00/6.00) rather than converting [2].
- Experience: graduates need internships and leadership roles; experienced hires 2+ years of
  strategy work (M3) or 3–7 years with project responsibility (M1). Corporate strategy,
  corporate development and transformation roles count (M3).
- Signals CaseCoach names: top academics and numeracy, a track record of strong performance,
  people skills and impact; leadership roles are "the main way firms differentiate" similar
  candidates [10]. Show two or three strongest roles in depth rather than listing everything.
- Tools: PowerPoint and Excel at expert level (M1, M3), Miro (M1), financial modelling (M4),
  generative AI familiarity as a plus (M3).
- Languages: fluent German and English (M1, M4); zeb asks for German C1 and English B2 (M2).
- Certificates: rarely decisive. Test scores (GMAT, GRE) where the firm asks [9].

### Section order and what goes first

- Graduates and early career: Header, short Profil, Ausbildung (with grades, exchange
  semesters, awards), Berufserfahrung (internships), Leadership and extracurricular roles,
  Sprachen and tools, interests. "Lead with education" [11].
- Experienced hires (3+ years): Header, Profil with the claim and the biggest proof,
  Kernkompetenzen (for example problem solving, client leadership, sector depth),
  Berufserfahrung, Ausbildung, languages, tools. "Lead with experience" [11].
- Put one area of depth on the page (a sector, a function or a demanding activity outside
  work) rather than only breadth.
- A one-page English version without photo is the safe choice for global firms; a two-page
  German version with photo fits Swiss boutiques.

### Example bullets (fictional)

- Structured a pricing review for a mid-sized retailer into five workstreams; led two and
  presented the recommendations to the executive board.
- Built a market model for three European countries that sized an entry option at CHF 40
  million annual revenue; the client approved a pilot.
- Identified CHF 6 million of annual procurement savings across 12 spend categories through
  spend analysis and 25 supplier interviews.
- Designed the target operating model for a merged finance function (180 staff) and planned
  the transition in three waves.
- Co-authored three proposals; two were won with a combined fee of CHF 1.5 million.
- Kostensenkungsprogramm für einen Industriekunden strukturiert; Einsparpotenzial von CHF 4 Mio.
  pro Jahr identifiziert und dem Verwaltungsrat präsentiert.
- Workstream «Zielbetriebsmodell» mit drei Beratenden geleitet; Umsetzung in 9 statt 12 Monaten.

## 2. IT and technology consulting

Covers Accenture, Deloitte, EY, IBM Consulting, Capgemini and Swiss IT consultancies (for
example adesso Schweiz, Swisscom's consulting units, SAP and ServiceNow partners). The work
sits between business and technology: target architectures, roadmaps, implementations.

### What recruiters expect

- Education: Master's in (business) informatics or a STEM field (I1, I2); ETH, university or
  FH degree, or equivalent experience (I3, I4). Swiss tertiary titles (FH, HF) are accepted in
  implementation roles.
- Experience: 2+ years in IT programmes or strategy consulting (I1); 3–5 years in software
  development, cloud or DevOps for people moving into advisory (I2); several years of
  platform consulting (I3, I4).
- Certificates matter more than in strategy consulting: platform certificates (ServiceNow
  Certified System Administrator, I3; Avaloq certification, I4), method certificates
  (PRINCE2, PMI, Scrum, I3), TOGAF as an advantage (I2). Standards: ITIL, BPMN (I3).
- Tools and topics: enterprise architecture, agile operating model, IT security (I1); SQL and
  JavaScript for platform roles (I3); cloud, application modernisation, DevOps (I2).
- Industry knowledge: banking processes such as payments or lending (I4).
- Languages: German and English in all four ads; French accepted instead of German at
  Deloitte (I2).
- Recruiters also want a person who can "bridge business and technology" (I2): explaining
  trade-offs to non-technical stakeholders.

### Section order and what goes first

- Header with target role and the one or two certificates the ad names (Accenture suggests
  certifications and credentials near the top [13]).
- Profil: years, platform or domain, one delivery proof with a number.
- Kernkompetenzen: typically one domain (for example banking), one platform or architecture
  strength, one consulting strength (workshops, stakeholder management).
- Berufserfahrung: for consultancies, group by employer and show two to four projects as
  points (client type, role, scope, result). Long project lists go into an optional
  project annex (Projektliste), which is common for IT consultants in DACH.
- Zertifikate directly after experience, then Ausbildung, languages, tools grouped by topic.

### Example bullets (fictional)

- Defined the target architecture and a three-year roadmap for a utility's landscape of 140
  applications; planned licence spend fell by 18 %.
- Led 14 requirement workshops with business and IT and turned the results into a solution
  design approved at the first steering committee.
- Migrated 60 workloads to a public cloud platform without unplanned downtime, as technical
  lead in a team of six.
- Introduced Scrum with two-week sprints across four teams; release frequency rose from
  quarterly to monthly.
- Wrote the value case for a CRM replacement (CHF 2.4 million investment, payback in three
  years) used for the board decision.
- ServiceNow-ITSM für einen Finanzdienstleister mit 3'000 Nutzenden eingeführt;
  Bearbeitungszeit pro Ticket um 30 % gesenkt.
- Fachkonzept und Parametrierung im Zahlungsverkehr einer Regionalbank verantwortet; Go-live
  termingerecht nach 10 Monaten.

## 3. Presales and sales engineering (solution consulting)

Covers solution consultants, sales engineers and presales architects at software, cloud and
security vendors and at Swiss integrators. The role pairs with an account executive: the
presales person wins the technical decision, the account executive the commercial one.

### What recruiters expect

- Experience: years of presales or solution consulting, from "mehrjährig" (P2, P4) to about
  10 years for senior advisory roles (P1). Sector depth, often financial services (P1, P2).
- The core cycle: discovery, tailored demos, proof of concept or value (PoC/PoV), RFI/RFP
  answers, effort estimates, and a clean handover to delivery (P1, P2, P4).
- Selling value, not features: ServiceNow asks to "sell the value of our solution, not just the
  features" (P1). Quarterly and annual sales goals and territory pipeline apply to presales too
  (P1).
- Technical proof: SQL and data architecture (P3), Python as a plus (P3); private cloud,
  Azure, Kubernetes, and a mandatory Azure certification (P4).
- Presenting to technical and executive audiences, "whether impromptu on a whiteboard" or with
  slides and demos (P3). C-suite contact (P1).
- Feeding field insight back to product and marketing (P1, P2, P3).
- Education: informatics, business informatics, business administration, or a higher
  professional qualification (höhere Fachausbildung, P2).
- Languages: German and English (P2, P3, P4); French and Italian as a plus (P2, P3).
- For career changers, PreSales Collective advises reframing duties as value, backed by as many
  numbers as possible [14].

### Section order and what goes first

- Header claim names the technical area and the business side, for example "Solution
  consultant for data platforms in financial services".
- Profil with a presales metric up front: win rate, influenced revenue or number of PoCs.
- Kernkompetenzen: one technical (platform, architecture), one sales (discovery, demo, RFP),
  one domain (sector and its regulation).
- Zertifikate next to or right after the strengths when the ad names one (P4 makes it
  mandatory).
- Berufserfahrung with numbers per role; then tools grouped (platforms, data, cloud, CRM),
  Ausbildung, languages.

### Example bullets (fictional)

- Ran 45 tailored demos and 8 proofs of concept in one fiscal year; 6 of 8 converted to closed
  deals worth CHF 3.1 million total contract value.
- Raised the technical win rate in the financial-services territory from 50 % to 68 % within
  two years.
- Coordinated answers to 12 RFPs with product, legal and delivery teams; shortlisted in 9.
- Built a reusable demo environment that cut preparation per demo from two days to half a day.
- Trained 15 account executives in discovery questions for a new data module.

## 4. Account management and B2B sales

Covers key account managers, account executives, business development managers and field
sales in software, industry and consumer goods. Numbers decide here more than anywhere else in
this Branche.

### What recruiters expect

- Education: university degree in business, economics or marketing, or equivalent (A1); in
  Switzerland also a commercial apprenticeship (kaufmännische Grundausbildung) with further
  education (A4) or a technical or commercial training plus FH, HF or TS level (A3). Sales,
  negotiation or key account courses are an advantage (A1).
- Experience: at least 4 years in the sector and 2 in key account management (A1); 5 years in
  similar commercial roles (A4); proven success in opening new markets (A3). Sector fit is
  often explicit (FMCG, spirits, machinery, IT infrastructure).
- Proof of results: quotas (A2), budgets, commercial KPIs and forecasting (A1, A4), renewals,
  cross- and upselling (A3, A4).
- Methods and tools: account plans (A1, A2, A4), business cases with ROI and TCO at CXO level
  (A2), MEDDICC or a similar qualification method (A2; the letters stand for metrics, economic
  buyer, decision criteria, decision process, identify pain, champion, competition [16]), CRM
  (A3), go-to-market plans (A3).
- Partners: distributors and channel partners (A2, A3); travel about 20 % (A3).
- Languages: fluent German (A2), German at native level (A4), German and English (A1, A3);
  French or Italian as a plus.
- Recognition counts: a sales award or ranking is a strong signal and should sit in the role
  where it was earned, with year and quota percentage [15]. Promotions should be visible
  (for example "promoted twice in four years") [15].

- Header claim with sector and segment, for example "Key account manager, Swiss retail,
  FMCG".
- Profil with the headline number (quota percentage, revenue grown, ranking).

  the base guide's three Kernkompetenzen can carry them.
- Berufserfahrung: for each role give the portfolio first (number of accounts, territory,
  segment, product), then two to four result points.
- Ausbildung and Weiterbildung (sales and negotiation courses), languages, CRM and tools,
  interests.

## Notes for the designer

- The claim and three proof numbers carry this Branche. Give the headline and a key-figures
  row (three numbers with a short label and year) the strongest place on page 1, above the
  experience. Numbers in tabular figures, so they align.
- Consulting: restrained and classic. Single column, one typeface, no logos, icons, charts or
  colour blocks; white space and clear headings do the work [7][10].
- IT consulting and presales: the same clean base, plus a visible certificate line near the
  top and tools grouped as plain text labels, never as skill bars or dots [1].
- Sales: may use a stronger accent colour for the key figures, but the text must stay
  ATS-readable: no contact details only in the page header, no layout tables [1].
- Photo as a switch per application: on for most German-language Swiss applications, off for
  English applications to global consulting firms and US vendors.
- One-page and two-page variants should come from the same data (a compact variant for
  consulting graduates).
