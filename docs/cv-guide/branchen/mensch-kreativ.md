# Mensch & Kreativ (HR, recruiting, design, communication)

Researched 2026-09-27 for the CV generator. Rules for CVs of other people in this Branche; `docs/cv-guide.md` stays the base rule set. Sources are listed at the end, numbered [1], [2] …

Job ads carry their own IDs (H1–H4, R1–R4, D1–D4, C1–C4) and are listed in each sub-sector's table, all seen 2026-09-27.

This Branche reads warm and personal: the profile may tell a short story, interests and
languages carry real weight, and collaboration is proven, not claimed. It is still a Swiss
CV: facts, dates and certificates come first, the personality comes through in the wording.

## How readers in this Branche read a CV

- The first screen decides. An eye-tracking study of 30 recruiters found an average of
  7.4 seconds per CV on the first pass; they read the current title and employer, the previous
  one, the dates, then the education [7]. Seniority, experience and functional knowledge must
  be visible at once [9].
- Simple layouts with clear headings and bullet points held attention; cluttered pages,
  multiple columns and long sentences lost it [7].
- Many employers in this Branche screen through an applicant tracking system (ATS) first.
  jobs.ch advises using the ad's keywords (titles, tools, certificates) in natural context,
  avoiding graphics-heavy layouts, and remembering that a person makes the decision [6].
- HR and recruiting readers are HR people themselves: they know the Swiss certificates and
  check at once whether the eidg. Fachausweis the ad asks for is there (H2, H3, R1).
  Recruiters look for specific experience, named systems and language skills [9].
- For design hiring managers the portfolio often matters more than the CV; the CV is the index
  to it [17]. NN/g surveyed more than 200 UX hiring managers: expectations differ for designers
  and researchers and for junior and senior roles [16].
- Communication readers treat the CV as a writing sample: grammar and spelling errors signal a
  lack of care [8], and ads ask for proven writing skill (C1, C4).
- Swiss ads in this Branche are often bilingual in their demands: German plus French, English
  or Italian appear as must or plus in 11 of the 16 ads below. The language section is read,
  not skipped.

## Common to all sub-sectors

### Length and format

- Two A4 pages is the target; Swiss career services set two to three pages as the ceiling [2].
  Designers may go shorter ("one page-ish" [17]) because the portfolio carries the detail.
- Photo: in Switzerland a CV photo is still usual [5]; university career services call it
  optional but, if used, professional, recent and head-and-shoulders [2][4]. In this people
  Branche a friendly, natural photo fits well (base rule in `docs/cv-guide.md` applies). US
  design guidance advises against headshots for bias reasons [17]; for an English CV to a
  US-led company, leave the choice to the person.
- Dates: `MM.YYYY – MM.YYYY`, newest first, gaps explained or left out deliberately [2].
- Languages: mother tongue first [3], then the others with the base guide's levels; name a
  CEFR level only with a certificate. French is often a must (R1, R2, D4), sometimes with a
  level (B2 in the armasuisse recruiter ad).
- Links: LinkedIn for everyone; a portfolio URL for designers (in the header, next to the
  name); writing samples or a clippings page for communication roles. Links must work without
  login and be short enough to type from paper.
- Personal data (date of birth, nationality, permit) is expected in a Swiss dossier [2][4] but
  this project publishes none of it by default (`docs/source-of-truth.md`). Some agency ads
  still name an age range (R3, R4); never shape a CV around it.
- File: PDF when sent, HTML while working (CLAUDE.md). Keep one ATS-safe column for the
  main text [6].

| Position | Change | Why |

| Kernkompetenzen | One of the three should prove collaboration or advising (stakeholders, managers, clients) | Nearly every ad below asks for it |

### Tone and verbs

- Impersonal, no "I" [1][8]. German bullets may use the nominal style common in Swiss CVs
  ("Beratung von Führungskräften in allen HR-Fragen") or start with a verb; keep one style per
  CV.
- Warm does not mean vague: "people person" or "kommunikativ" are claims; an example of a
  mediated conflict or a trained team is proof.
- Harvard verbs [1] that fit, by sub-sector:

| Sub-sector | Harvard verbs that fit | Use with care | Extra verbs typical here |
|---|---|---|---|
| HR | Advised, Coached, Facilitated, Mediated, Negotiated, Implemented, Standardized, Streamlined, Guided, Trained, Administered, Processed, Reconciled | Arbitrated (only formal arbitration), Persuaded, Convinced (sound manipulative about employees), Supervised (only with line responsibility) | Onboarded, Partnered with, Supported (sparingly), Handled (cases), Introduced (a process) |
| Recruiting | Recruited, Screened, Interviewed, Negotiated, Selected, Coordinated, Advised, Built, Increased, Reduced | Sold (only agency roles), Spearheaded (needs a lead role) | Sourced, Placed, Filled, Hired, Pitched (agency), Won (clients, agency) |
| Design and UX | Designed, Redesigned, Conceptualized, Visualized, Illustrated, Created, Tested, Interviewed, Standardized, Collaborated | Engineered, Programmed (only with real code), Invented | Prototyped, Iterated, Validated, Shipped, Art-directed |
| Communication | Wrote, Edited, Authored, Drafted, Published, Presented, Publicized, Promoted, Moderated, Translated, Launched | Energized, Influenced (vague without an outcome) | Pitched (to media), Positioned, Briefed, Produced (video) |

- Avoid Technical verbs that do not fit people work (Fabricated, Overhauled, Operated) and
  Helping verbs with a medical sound (Diagnosed, Rehabilitated) in HR texts.

individual cases and anything that identifies employees never do.

### What to avoid

- Confidential people data: no names, health cases or salary figures of employees or
  candidates, not even anonymised if the case is recognisable.
- Skill bars and star ratings: subjective and unreadable for ATS [6][17].
- A "too designy" CV that hides the content [17]; a list of soft skills without proof.
- Hobbies copied from a list; mention only what the person can talk about [2][3].
- Lying or inflating titles: recruiters verify diplomas and experience [8].
- For designers: client work under NDA shown openly; anonymise or describe it [15].

## HR (HR business partner, HR generalist, people & culture, L&D, payroll)

### What recruiters expect

- Certificate: HR-Fachfrau/-mann mit eidg. Fachausweis is the standard entry ticket; ads say
  "zwingend" (H3) or "mindestens auf Stufe HR-Fachausweis" (H2). The Fachrichtung (betriebliches Human
  Resource Management, öffentliche Personalvermittlung und -beratung, or private
  Personalvermittlung und -verleih) should be named [11]. Above it: Leiter/in Human Resources
  with eidg. Diplom (HFP); below it: HR-Assistent/in with HRSE certificate [11][12].
- Basic education: kaufmännische Grundausbildung (EFZ) or a degree, then the HR training (H3, H4).
- Knowledge: Swiss labour law (Arbeitsrecht, OR) and social insurance
  (Sozialversicherungen: AHV/IV/EO, ALV, BVG, UVG, KTG) (H3, H4); payroll (Lohnbuchhaltung,
  Lohnverarbeitung) for generalists and payroll roles (H3, H4).
- Tools: HRIS and payroll systems by name: SAP SuccessFactors (H1, H3), UKG (H1, H3),
  Abacus (H4), MS Office (H1).
- Experience: "several years" in a comparable people function (H1, H2), often in complex or
  public organisations (H2).
- Soft skills the ads spell out: empathy, trust, calm under pressure, structured and exact
  work, humour (H1, H2, H3).
- International HR certificates (SHRM-CP/SCP, CIPD) count for English-language roles; spell
  out the full name once for the ATS [10].

### Section order and what goes first

1. Header with the title including the certificate, if the person holds it.
2. Profil: HR focus (generalist, HRBP, payroll, L&D), years, sector, size of the population
   supported, and one sentence on how the person works with people.
3. Kernkompetenzen: typically advising managers, Swiss payroll and social insurance, and one
   process or change project.
4. Berufserfahrung with scope per position (employees supported, sites, languages).
5. Ausbildung & Weiterbildung, Fachausweis first; for L&D roles add an adult-education trainer
   certificate (SVEB-Zertifikat) only if the person has it.
6. Sprachen, then Weitere Kenntnisse grouped as HRIS/payroll, law and insurance, methods.

## Recruiting and talent acquisition (in-house, sourcer, agency / Personalberater)

### What recruiters expect

- In-house: eidg. HR-Fachausweis or comparable (R1, and in the armasuisse and Staar Surgical
  ads seen); two to three years of end-to-end recruiting (R1, R2), five for senior roles
  (medmix); active sourcing, often named as LinkedIn Recruiter (R2); stakeholder management with hiring managers (R2); candidate experience and employer
  branding (R1, R2); recruiting data and KPIs.
- Tools: an ATS by name (SuccessFactors, Workday, Personio, Abacus or similar); job boards
  (jobs.ch, LinkedIn); the ads seen ask for "applicant tracking systems" in general, so the CV
  should name the exact system used.
- Agency recruiting (Personalberater/in) behaves like sales. The ads ask for sales experience
  (Verkaufserfahrung) and negotiation skill (R3, R4), business development, client care,
  closing, and temporary as well as permanent placement (R4). The CV then needs sales
  numbers: placements, clients won, gross margin or hours placed, not only hires.
- Swiss agency context: private placement and staffing need a cantonal licence under the
  Recruitment Act (AVG) [13]; the HR-Fachausweis has a Fachrichtung for private placement and
  staffing [11], and HRSE offers a Staffing HR-Assistent/in certificate [12]. Experience with
  the staffing GAV (Personalverleih) is worth naming for agency roles.
- Regional roots and a network count in agency ads (R4); R3 asks for a driving licence.
- Languages: German plus French are common (R1, R2); English for international talent.

### Section order and what goes first

- In-house: Profil with the recruiting scope (roles, levels, regions, hires per year), then
  Kernkompetenzen such as sourcing, stakeholder advice, process or employer branding. Tools
  (ATS, LinkedIn Recruiter) in Weitere Kenntnisse and once in the relevant bullet.
- Agency: Profil like a sales profile (sector focus, client base, placement type), then
  Kernkompetenzen with one commercial strength (client acquisition) and one people strength
  (matching, candidate care). Lead bullets with the number where possible.
- Sourcers: lead with search techniques (Boolean search, LinkedIn Recruiter, talent mapping)
  and pipeline figures.

## Creative roles (graphic and visual design, UX/UI design)

### What recruiters expect

- A portfolio, always: ads ask for "vollständige Bewerbungsunterlagen, inkl. Portfolio" (D4);
  design leaders call the portfolio "king" [17]. For UX, three to five case studies, each with
  problem, own role, process, rejected ideas, outcome and learning [14].
- Education: graphic design routes are Grafiker/in EFZ, Polygraf/in EFZ, Mediamatiker/in EFZ
  (D4), then HF (dipl. Kommunikationsdesigner/in HF) or a BA/MA in design; UX ads ask for a
  degree in UX/UI, interaction, service, media or graphic design, or computer science (D1, D2).
- Tools: Figma (D1, D2, D3); Adobe Creative Cloud with InDesign, Illustrator, Photoshop
  (D1, D3, D4); design systems and component libraries (D1, D3); generative AI tools such as
  Midjourney or Adobe Firefly now appear as requirements (D3).
- UX methods: UX research, usability tests, validation (D2); prototyping (D1); HTML/CSS basics
  as a plus (D2); UX writing or content design as a plus (D2).
- Brand work: corporate identity, brand guidelines, print and digital channels, briefing
  photographers and printers (D3, D4).
- Experience: at least three years in product UI (D1), "mehrjährig" in brand or visual
  communication (D3, D4).
- Expectations differ for junior and senior designers and for designers and researchers [16].

### Section order and what goes first

1. Header: name, target role, portfolio URL (and LinkedIn). The portfolio link is the most
   important line on the page.
2. Profil: discipline, years, type of products or brands, one sentence on how the person
   works with others (research, developers, clients).
3. Kernkompetenzen: for example UX research, design systems, brand identity; each points to a
   case study in the portfolio.
4. Berufserfahrung: each position may end with "Case study: <short title>" pointing to the
   portfolio instead of long descriptions.
5. Ausbildung, then Weitere Kenntnisse grouped as tools, methods and code.
6. The CV itself shows the person can design, but stays readable and ATS-safe [6][17].

## Communication, marketing and content

### What recruiters expect

- Education: a degree or training in communication, journalism, PR, media or business
  (C1, C2, C3, C4). Swiss vocational route: Kommunikationsfachfrau/-mann with eidg.
  Fachausweis, then Kommunikationsleiter/in or Marketingleiter/in HFP [18]; the
  PR-Fachausweis (PR-Fachfrau/-mann) requires a prior Fachausweis such as this one.
- Writing: "sichere Schreibkompetenz", storytelling (C1), clear, target-group-oriented texts
  on complex topics (C4). Native-level German is often a must (C1, C3).
- Corporate communications: media relations and knowledge of the Swiss media landscape (C2, C4),
  internal communication (C2, C4), crisis communication (C2), executive positioning (C4).
- Social media and content: channel strategy, community management, content creation,
  organic and paid social, metrics and optimisation (C1, C3); newsletter, SEO, CMS as a plus (C3).
- Tools: CMS (WordPress, TYPO3, Adobe Experience Manager), Google Analytics, social
  listening, AI-supported tools (C2); Adobe Creative Cloud and Canva (C1, C2).
- Public sector: government communication (Behördenkommunikation) and interest in politics
  (C1).
- Experience ranges from unquantified (C1, C3) to 5+ years (C4) or 7+ years with leadership
  (C2).

## Notes for the designer

- Warm but orderly: generous white space, one readable main column, a friendly photo on
  page 1 [5][7].
- HR and recruiting: the certificate and the languages should be findable in one glance
  (header title, clear Sprachen block). Numbers in recruiting bullets may be set in tabular
  figures so they line up.
- Design and UX: the portfolio link is the strongest element after the name; allow an
  optional row of up to three project titles linking to case studies. No skill bars [17].
- Communication: typography carries the tone; the profile may be slightly larger as a lead
  paragraph. Allow a "Selected work" link list.
- Interests are a real section here, not a footnote; give them room at the end of page 2.
